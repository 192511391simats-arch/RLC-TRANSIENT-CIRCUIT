Transient Analysis of a Series RLC Circuit in a Power Supply Unit

When a power supply is switched on, its inductor and capacitor do not settle instantly. A second-order differential equation predicts the surge current and the voltage overshoot. This site solves that equation exactly and lets you explore it.

Project overview

A power supply unit (PSU) connects its output filter to a DC source through a switch. The resistance, inductance and capacitance in that path form a series RLC circuit. This project models the first few milliseconds after switch-on, with the circuit starting at rest, and classifies the behavior as underdamped, critically damped or overdamped.

Objectives

  • Derive and solve L·i″ + R·i′ + i/C = 0 for a DC step with i(0) = 0 and vC(0) = 0.
  • Compute ωn, α, ζ and Rcritical from user-defined component values.
  • Plot exact current and capacitor-voltage responses, with peaks and settling time.
  • Compare the three damping regimes and relate them to real PSU design.

Mathematical Analysis

The general theory, then a worked calculation using the values currently entered in the simulator.

1. Governing equation

Kirchhoff's voltage law around the loop, after the switch closes at t = 0 with a constant source V:

V = R·i + L·di/dt + vC,  i = C·dvC/dt

Differentiating once removes the constant source and leaves the homogeneous equation for t > 0:

L·d²i/dt² + R·di/dt + i/C = 0

Initial conditions for an unenergized circuit. The inductor current cannot jump, and at t = 0⁺ the capacitor is still at 0 V, so the full source voltage sits across L:

i(0) = 0    di/dt(0⁺) = V/L    vC(0) = 0

2. Characteristic equation and parameters

s² + 2α·s + ωn² = 0  →  s = −α ± √(α² − ωn²)
ωn = 1/√(LC)    α = R/(2L)    ζ = α/ωn = (R/2)·√(C/L)
Rcritical = 2·√(L/C)    (ζ = 1 when R = Rcritical)

ζ < 1 gives complex roots (oscillation), ζ = 1 a double real root, and ζ > 1 two distinct real roots. R = 0 is the lossless limit (ζ = 0).

3. Step-response solutions

Underdamped (ωd = √(ωn² − α²)):

i(t) = (V/(L·ωd))·e−αt·sin(ωdt)
vC(t) = V·[1 − e−αt(cos ωdt + (α/ωd)·sin ωdt)]

Critically damped:

i(t) = (V/L)·t·e−αt
vC(t) = V·[1 − (1 + αt)·e−αt]

Overdamped (β = √(α² − ωn²), s₁ = −α + β, s₂ = −α − β):

i(t) = (V/(2βL))·(es₁t − es₂t)
vC(t) = V·[1 + (s₂es₁t − s₁es₂t)/(s₁ − s₂)]

4. Peaks and settling

Under: tpk,i = atan(ωd/α)/ωd,   tpk,v = π/ωd
vC,max = V·(1 + e−απ/ωd)
Critical: tpk,i = 1/α,   imax = 2V/(e·R)
Over: tpk,i = ln((α+β)/ωn)/β

Critically damped and overdamped capacitor voltages rise monotonically to V with no overshoot, so their peak equals the final value. Settling time is the last instant vC leaves a ±2 % band around V, found numerically from the exact solution. For underdamped circuits, 4/α is a quick envelope estimate.

    Underdamped

    Fastest first rise, but vC overshoots V and rings. Low resistance means little energy is lost per cycle.

    Critically damped

    The quickest approach to V with no overshoot. It is the design boundary between ringing and sluggishness.

    Overdamped

    No overshoot, but the slow real root makes it creep toward V. Extra resistance also dissipates more power.

    Applications & Conclusion

    Why a second-order ODE matters for real power electronics.

    Switch-on surge

    The peak current found here sizes fuses, switches and rectifier diodes.

    Overshoot protection

    A voltage peak above V can exceed the rating of downstream capacitors and ICs.

    Filter design

    LC output filters in buck converters and EMI filters are tuned using ζ and ωn.

    Start-up time

    Settling time sets how soon a regulated rail is ready for its load.

    Conclusion

    • The step response of a series RLC circuit is fully set by ζ = (R/2)·√(C/L), compared with 1 (R versus Rcritical = 2√(L/C)).
    • Low damping gives fast response with overshoot up to 2V in the lossless limit. High damping removes overshoot at the price of speed.
    • Critical damping is the fastest non-overshooting response, which is why designers aim near ζ ≈ 0.7–1 in practice.
    • Every graph here uses the closed-form solutions with i(0) = 0 and vC(0) = 0, so values can be checked by hand.
    • Limits: ideal components, a constant source, and no parasitics such as ESR or switch resistance, which a full PSU model would add.
